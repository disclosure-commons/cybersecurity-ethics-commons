Taxonomy

The categories and tags used to classify every case in the commons — the codebook behind the dataset.

Primary categories

Every case is assigned exactly one primary category, chosen for its central ethical tension even when other themes are present.

Vulnerability Disclosure

Cases centered on when, how, and to whom to disclose security findings — including responsible disclosure, media-first disclosure, non-responsive vendors, and provocation tactics.

13 cases

Informed Consent & Human Subjects

Research conducted on people or communities without their knowledge or consent — phishing simulations, passive traffic monitoring, Tor de-anonymization, honeypots.

7 cases

Dual-Use Research

Work that produces knowledge, tools, or methods usable for both defense and attack — ransomware simulations, keylogger research, internet-wide scanning, mass vulnerability databases.

11 cases

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach

Cases where good-faith or ambiguous research collided with criminal law, often highlighting disproportionate prosecution or vague statutory language (CFAA, DMCA, CMA).

24 cases

Institutional Suppression

Cases where vendors, governments, or employers used legal, political, or economic power to silence, suppress, or retaliate against researchers exposing flaws or breaches.

18 cases

Financial Conflict of Interest

Cases where research or disclosure was motivated or tainted by profit — short-selling, bug bounty disputes, threat-intelligence monetization, nonprofit commercial data deals.

6 cases

Critical Infrastructure & Public Trust

Research on elections, medical devices, transit, water systems, and power grids where findings carry societal risk beyond the technical, including potential to fuel disinformation.

8 cases

Surveillance & Offensive Tools

Development, sale, or deployment of tools designed to compromise systems at scale — commercial spyware, government malware operations, cyber-mercenary firms.

6 cases
