Case No.013

University of Michigan (e-Voting)

Critical Infrastructure & Public Trust · 2010.0

A Michigan security team authorized by DC election officials hacked a Washington DC internet voting pilot within 36 hours, gaining full control and modifying vote totals. DC cancelled the system; the team published full technical details.

Academic researcher
Good faith (intended public benefit)
Responsible/coordinated
No charges filed

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Does authorized pentesting of election infrastructure risk undermining public trust in democracy?; (ii) Full technical disclosure vs. limiting details that could enable copycat attacks; (iii) Academic freedom to publish results of authorized civic hacking; (iv) Coordination challenges between researchers, vendors, and election officials
