Case No.056

Bernstein v. US Dept. of State

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 1995
Chilling effect

Daniel Bernstein challenged U.S. export regulations that required government approval before he could publish his encryption algorithm, Snuffle, and its source code. Bernstein argued that these restrictions violated his First Amendment rights by treating academic research and computer code as regulated exports. In 1999, a Ninth Circuit panel ruled in his favor, recognizing encryption source code as protected speech. However, the opinion was subsequently withdrawn for rehearing, and the case was remanded following changes to export regulations, leaving the broader constitutional questions unresolved.

Academic researcher
Good faith (intended public benefit)
None (withheld)
Civil challenge; panel opinion withdrawn and case remanded

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Is encryption source code 'speech' protected by the First Amendment, or purely functional 'conduct' that can be regulated without First Amendment scrutiny?; (ii) Can the government require a license before a researcher publishes an academic paper containing functional encryption code?; (iii) Do national security interests in controlling encryption export justify prior restraint on academic publication?; (iv) How should the law treat dual-use academic research that has both civilian security value and potential military/intelligence applications?
