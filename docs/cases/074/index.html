Case No.074

DJI / Researcher Bug Bounty Quarrel (Kevin Finisterre — extended / SecurityWeek)

Vulnerability Disclosure · 2017.0
Chilling effect

Security researcher Kevin Finisterre found DJI's wildcard SSL certificate and AWS S3 bucket access keys exposed on GitHub, potentially exposing customer flight logs, passports, and ID documents. When he reported through DJI's newly launched bug bounty program, DJI allegedly threatened CFAA prosecution and demanded he sign a restrictive NDA before receiving the $30,000 bounty. DJI disputed Finisterre's characterization. Finisterre declined the money and published his account publicly. The case became a template for how bug bounty programs can be used to silence researchers rather than reward them.

Independent / contractor
Good faith (intended public benefit)
Responsible/coordinated
No charges filed

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Can a bug bounty program's NDA terms be used to effectively suppress a researcher's ability to discuss findings that are already known to the company?; (ii) Is threatening CFAA prosecution against a researcher who operated within a company's own published bug bounty scope an abuse of the legal process?; (iii) What power imbalances exist between corporate legal teams and individual researchers, and how should bug bounty programs be regulated to address them?; (iv) Does a vendor's threat of prosecution after the fact constitute retroactive withdrawal of implied authorization to test?
