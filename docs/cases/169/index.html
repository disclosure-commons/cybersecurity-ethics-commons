Case No.169

Xiaofeng Wang / Indiana University Federal Investigation

Institutional Suppression · 2025
Chilling effect

In March 2025, federal agents searched homes associated with Indiana University cybersecurity professor Xiaofeng Wang and his wife, Nianli Ma, while the university terminated their employment. Reporting described a separate institutional inquiry into possible research-funding disclosures, but public evidence did not establish wrongdoing. Their lawyers said they had not been arrested or charged. The events raised questions about academic due process, research collaboration, and the presumption of innocence.

Academic researcher
Ambiguous / gray area (mixed or unclear intent)
None (withheld)
Federal investigation reported; no criminal charges publicly announced at the time of reporting

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) How should universities safeguard due process while responding to federal investigations?; (ii) When does scrutiny of international research funding risk stigmatizing legitimate collaboration?; (iii) What information should institutions disclose when allegations remain unproven?; (iv) How can research-integrity procedures avoid causing disproportionate reputational harm?
