Case No.007

Carnegie Mellon University (CMU / SEI)

Informed Consent & Human Subjects · 2014
Chilling effect

In 2014, the Tor Project identified a traffic-confirmation attack involving malicious Tor relays, while a related Carnegie Mellon University presentation about identifying Tor users was withdrawn from Black Hat. Allegations of law-enforcement involvement raised questions about research authorization, user consent, and disclosure; the precise relationship between the work and later investigations was contested.

Academic researcher
Ambiguous / gray area (mixed or unclear intent)
None (withheld)
No charges filed

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Research on human subjects without consent; (ii) Withholding a vulnerability from the affected party (Tor Project); (iii) Financial incentives vs. ethical responsibility; (iv) Using academic research as outsourced law enforcement
