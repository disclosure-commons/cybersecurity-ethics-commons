Case No.059

Matthew Green & bunnie Huang v. DOJ (DMCA / Section 1201)

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 2016–2024
Chilling effect

Johns Hopkins cryptographer Matthew Green and engineer Andrew 'bunnie' Huang filed a First Amendment challenge (via EFF) against Section 1201 of the DMCA, arguing it unconstitutionally prevented Green from publishing security research on commercial devices and Huang from building a legitimate video-stream device. Green said DMCA fear caused him to decline to investigate certain devices and chilled him from disclosing vulnerabilities. The DC Circuit ultimately upheld the DMCA's anti-circumvention provisions in 2024, finding they regulate conduct not speech.

Academic researcher
Good faith (intended public benefit)
None (withheld)
Civil suit / Injunction

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Does the DMCA's anti-circumvention provision unconstitutionally chill security research that requires bypassing DRM?; (ii) Should researchers be required to apply for triennial exemptions to do work that would otherwise be protected fair use?; (iii) How does the chilling effect of potential prosecution shape what security vulnerabilities get investigated and disclosed?; (iv) When researchers self-censor to avoid legal risk, who bears the cost in terms of undetected vulnerabilities?
