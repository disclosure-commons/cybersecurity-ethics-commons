Case No.063

AFGE v. OPM (Federal Employee / Security Clearance Data Breach)

Critical Infrastructure & Public Trust · 2015–2019

The Office of Personnel Management (OPM) suffered a breach in 2014–2015 that exposed background investigation files of 21.5 million current and former federal employees, including SF-86 security clearance forms with deeply sensitive personal information. OPM had been warned repeatedly about its poor cybersecurity posture but failed to act. The American Federation of Government Employees sued; courts ultimately dismissed on standing grounds, but the case raised profound questions about government accountability for protecting its employees' most sensitive data.

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) What duty of care does a government agency owe its employees when it holds their most sensitive personal and national security data?; (ii) When repeated security warnings are ignored and a catastrophic breach occurs, who bears personal accountability?; (iii) Should federal employees whose security clearance files were stolen have a right to sue for damages?; (iv) Does sovereign immunity effectively shield government agencies from accountability for catastrophic security failures?
