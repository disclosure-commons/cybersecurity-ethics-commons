Case No.084

FreeHour / Malta Students (Grigolo, Debono, Scerri, Collins)

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 2022–2023
Chilling effect

Four computer-science students reported vulnerabilities in the FreeHour student timetabling app with a 90-day disclosure window and asked whether the report might qualify for a bounty once fixed. FreeHour went to police; three students had their homes searched, were strip-searched and had devices seized. The company said the mention of payment, changes to the app's front end and the 90-day 'ultimatum' led its lawyers to treat the report as a threat.

Student
Good faith (intended public benefit)
Responsible/coordinated
Arrested (devices seized; no conviction reported)

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Stating a disclosure deadline is standard practice, but paired with a payment question it can read as coercion: how should researchers state a timeline and ask about bounties without appearing to extort? (ii) Was altering the app's front end necessary to prove the flaw, or did it go beyond what proof required? (iii) Should a company with no disclosure policy be expected to recognize good-faith research, or is caution toward unsolicited reports reasonable?
