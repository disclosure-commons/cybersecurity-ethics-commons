Case No.040

Paige Thompson / Capital One / AWS

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 2019–2025

- former amazon web services software engineer who left AWS in 2016 - in 2019 she found a capital one web application firewall that was misconfigured - used it to trick the server into handing over temporary cloud credentials (a server-side request forgery attack) (verify the technique) - used those credentials to copy data stored in capital one’s AWS cloud storage ~100 million people in the US and ~6 million in canada were affected, including about 140,000 SSNs and 80,000 bank account numbers (verify numbers) - data from 30+ other organizations was also reportedly accessed, not just capital one - she posted about it on slack, twitter and github, which is how someone found out - a tipster emailed capital one’s responsible disclosure address in july 2019, capital one confirmed the breach and told the FBI-- - she was arrested within about 2 weeks of the tip - she also used some of the accessed servers for cryptomining (cryptojacking) - she said she was doing it to find and report vulnerabilities, prosecutors said she was after money and was bragging about it - jury convicted her in 2022 of wire fraud and 4 counts of unauthorized access to a protected computer, but not the identity theft or access device charges (verify counts) - sentenced to time served and 5 years of probation - capital one paid an $80 million regulatory fine and a $190 million class action settlement

Corporate / private sector
Malicious / reckless (knowingly harmful)
None (withheld)
Convicted (time served, supervised release, $40.7M restitution)

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Where is the line between legitimate cloud-misconfiguration research and felony data theft?; (ii) Should mental health and personal circumstances factor into sentencing for large-scale breaches?; (iii) Appellate oversight of 'too lenient' sentences for first-time offenders
