Case No.073

Microsoft / Nightmare Eclipse (Windows Defender & BitLocker Zero-Days)

Vulnerability Disclosure · 2026.0
Chilling effect

An anonymous researcher known as 'Nightmare Eclipse' publicly disclosed six unpatched Windows vulnerabilities — including flaws in Defender and BitLocker — after alleging that Microsoft deleted their MSRC (Security Response Center) account, withheld bounty payments, and removed their attribution from an advisory. Three of the six disclosed vulnerabilities (BlueHammer, RedSun, UnDefend) were subsequently exploited in real-world attacks and added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. Microsoft's Digital Crimes Unit publicly threatened criminal prosecution; the researcher was also banned from GitHub and GitLab. The cybersecurity community largely sided with the researcher, with veterans warning Microsoft's stance would chill future disclosure.

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) When a company allegedly mistreats a researcher — deleting their account, withholding bounty payments, removing attribution — does that researcher bear ethical responsibility for subsequently publishing unpatched vulnerabilities?; (ii) Is 'full disclosure' of an unpatched vulnerability ever ethically justified as a last resort against an unresponsive or hostile vendor?; (iii) Does threatening a researcher with criminal prosecution for disclosing bugs create a chilling effect that ultimately harms users more than the disclosure itself?; (iv) Who bears moral responsibility when a researcher-disclosed vulnerability is exploited in the wild — the researcher or the vendor that failed to patch it?
