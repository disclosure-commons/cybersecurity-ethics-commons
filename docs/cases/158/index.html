Case No.158

Edward Snowden / NSA surveillance disclosures

Institutional Suppression · 2013
Chilling effect

In 2013, former intelligence contractor Edward Snowden gave journalists classified documents revealing U.S. surveillance programs, including bulk collection of telephone metadata. The disclosures prompted major public debate and policy changes, while the U.S. government charged Snowden under the Espionage Act. The case raises questions about secrecy, public-interest reporting, and whistleblower protections.

Government / intelligence
Ambiguous / gray area (mixed or unclear intent)
Media-first / leak
Charges filed; Snowden remains in exile

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Is breaking secrecy obligations justified when the programs may be unlawful? (ii) Should he have used internal channels first? (iii) How should whistleblowers be protected, given there is no real public-interest defense under the Espionage Act?
