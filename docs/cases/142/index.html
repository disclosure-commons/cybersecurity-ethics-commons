Case No.142

Guillaume Tena (Guillermito) / Tegam Viguard

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 2001–2005
Chilling effect

Tena published research showing weaknesses in Tegam's Viguard antivirus, contradicting its marketing claims. Tegam pursued him in French courts, which imposed a suspended fine of 5,000 euros.

Independent / contractor
Good faith (intended public benefit)
Media-first
Convicted (suspended €5,000 fine)

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Should reverse engineering to test a security product's claims be treated as copyright infringement? (ii) Do consumers' interests in accurate antivirus marketing outweigh vendor IP? (iii) How should researchers in stricter jurisdictions publish product evaluations?
