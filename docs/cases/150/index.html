Case No.150

Apple v. FBI / San Bernardino iPhone

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 2015

Following the 2015 San Bernardino terrorist attack, the FBI sought an order requiring Apple to help unlock an attacker's iPhone without triggering its passcode protections. Apple opposed the order, arguing that creating the requested software would weaken user security more broadly. The government withdrew the request in March 2016 after gaining access through an outside party.

Government / law enforcement
Good faith (intended public benefit)
Public legal dispute
FBI withdrew court order after third-party access

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Can a company be forced to weaken its own security? (ii) One phone vs. every user’s security: what responsibility does the outside party that unlocked it have?
