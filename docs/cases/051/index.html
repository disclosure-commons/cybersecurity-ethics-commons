Case No.051

Clayton Taylor Dillard / Wake Internal Medicine (Wi-Fi)

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 2003.0
Chilling effect

An IT consultant accessed an unsecured medical practice wireless network, obtained patient billing and insurance records, and contacted the press and patients directly to publicize the clinic's lax security. He pleaded guilty to wireless computer intrusion — the first such conviction in the US.

Independent / contractor
Ambiguous / gray area (mixed or unclear intent)
Media-first
Convicted (plea)

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Why did near-identical facts (Puffer acquitted, Dillard convicted) produce opposite outcomes?; (ii) Contacting patients/press directly vs. notifying the practice first; (iii) Patient privacy harm from the disclosure method itself, separate from the underlying flaw
