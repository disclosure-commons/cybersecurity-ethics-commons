Case No.070

United States v. Verkada Inc. (Mass Surveillance Camera Breach)

Surveillance & Offensive Tools · 2021–2024

Hacker collective Tillie Kottmann breached Verkada, a cloud-based security camera company, accessing live feeds from 150,000+ cameras inside hospitals, prisons, Tesla factories, and schools. Kottmann claimed the breach was meant to expose the pervasiveness of mass surveillance. Verkada was later fined $2.95M by the FTC for inadequate security practices. Kottmann was indicted on conspiracy charges; the case raised questions about whether exposing surveillance infrastructure is a legitimate form of security activism.

Independent / contractor
Ambiguous / gray area (mixed or unclear intent)
Media-first
Indicted (Kottmann); FTC civil action against Verkada ($2.95M fine)

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Is breaching a surveillance company to expose its reach a form of protected political/security activism or straightforward criminal hacking?; (ii) Does the target being a surveillance company change the ethical calculus of an unauthorized breach?; (iii) What accountability should cloud security camera vendors face when their poor security exposes sensitive environments like hospitals and prisons?; (iv) When a vendor's negligence enables mass surveillance exposure, who bears moral responsibility — the hacker or the vendor?
