Case No.055

AFGE v. OPM (Federal Employee / Security Clearance Data Breach)

Critical Infrastructure & Public Trust · 2015

The 2014–2015 Office of Personnel Management breaches exposed sensitive information about more than 21 million people, including federal employees and applicants for security clearances. The American Federation of Government Employees and others brought suit over the government's protection of this data; a federal appeals court reinstated some claims after an initial dismissal.

Government / law enforcement
Ambiguous / gray area (mixed or unclear intent)
None (withheld)
Civil litigation; some claims reinstated on appeal

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) What duty of care does a government agency owe its employees when it holds their most sensitive personal and national security data?; (ii) When repeated security warnings are ignored and a catastrophic breach occurs, who bears personal accountability?; (iii) Should federal employees whose security clearance files were stolen have a right to sue for damages?; (iv) Does sovereign immunity effectively shield government agencies from accountability for catastrophic security failures?
