Case No.161

(FIX) Microsoft Exchange ProxyLogon exploitation

Vulnerability Disclosure · 2021

in early 2021 attackers (Microsoft attributed it to a China-linked group, Hafnium) exploited zero-day vulnerabilities in on-premises Exchange servers they planted web shells that gave persistent access, and other groups piled on after the vulnerabilities became public Microsoft released patches, but many organizations were slow to apply them or did not know they were compromised patching did not remove web shells that were already installed in April 2021 the DOJ got a court order to have the FBI copy and remove web shells from hundreds of vulnerable US servers owners did not consent beforehand and were notified afterward

Government / law enforcement
Ambiguous / gray area (mixed or unclear intent)
Vendor disclosure and later remediation
Mass exploitation and emergency remediation

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Should the government be able to access private systems without consent to fix a security problem? (ii) Who is responsible for cleanup when owners cannot or will not do it? (iii) Does a court order make it ethical if owners are not told first?
