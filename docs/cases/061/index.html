Case No.061

In Re Okta, Inc. Securities Litigation

Financial Conflict of Interest · 2022–2023

Okta suffered a breach by the Lapsus$ hacking group in January 2022 but did not publicly disclose it for over two months, and only after a hacker posted screenshots publicly. When Okta did disclose, executives initially downplayed the scope, later admitting that all Okta customer support users' data had been accessed. Shareholders filed a securities class action alleging Okta made materially false and misleading statements about the breach's scope and timing.

Corporate / private sector
Ambiguous / gray area (mixed or unclear intent)
None (withheld)
Civil suit / Injunction

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) When a company minimizes a breach publicly while knowing the full scope internally, is that fraud or legitimate incident response?; (ii) What is the appropriate disclosure timeline when breach scope is still being investigated?; (iii) Does downplaying a breach to avoid stock impact constitute securities fraud even if the company later corrects the record?; (iv) How should security executives balance legal counsel's advice against ethical obligations to customers?
