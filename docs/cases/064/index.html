Case No.064

hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn Corp.

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 2017–2022
Chilling effect

hiQ, a data analytics company, scraped public LinkedIn profiles using automated bots to build workforce analytics products. LinkedIn sent a cease-and-desist and blocked hiQ's access. hiQ sued; the Ninth Circuit twice ruled that scraping publicly accessible data likely does not violate the CFAA because 'without authorization' requires a gate/barrier the scraper bypassed. The Supreme Court vacated and remanded in light of Van Buren. Ultimately hiQ settled, agreeing to a $500K judgment and permanent injunction after LinkedIn won on breach-of-contract grounds using fake profile creation.

Corporate / private sector
Financially motivated (profit, stock, bounty)
None (withheld)
Civil suit / Injunction

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Is scraping publicly accessible data 'unauthorized access' under the CFAA if no password or authentication barrier was bypassed?; (ii) Can a company weaponize the CFAA to prevent a competitor from accessing data that users themselves made public?; (iii) Where is the line between lawful data collection for research/analysis and commercial exploitation of user-generated public data?; (iv) Does the CFAA's broad interpretation give large platforms de facto monopoly control over public data?
