Case No.100

Steven Jensen / Nerium International

Vulnerability Disclosure · 2016
Chilling effect

Jensen found an enumeration flaw in Nerium's customer portal that let any customer see others' details, including payment and address information. Nerium ignored his reports and contacted him only after he posted enough detail to prove the issue, with a cease-and-desist letter. He replaced the post with a timeline.

Independent / contractor
Good faith (intended public benefit)
Vendor non-responsive → public disclosure
Civil threat

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) How much detail must be public to force a non-responsive company to act, and how much is too much? (ii) Should researchers comply with a takedown demand when users remain exposed? (iii) Does exposing payment data change the duty to warn affected customers?
