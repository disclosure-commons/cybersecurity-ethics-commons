Case No.168

(FIX) Ashley Madison data breach

Informed Consent & Human Subjects · 2015

in July 2015 a group calling itself the Impact Team stole user data from Ashley Madison, a dating site for people seeking affairs they demanded that the parent company shut down the site, and published the data in August when it did not the leak exposed names, emails, and other personal details of millions of users users faced blackmail, public shaming, and reported harm to families and jobs the group framed itself as acting morally, objecting to the site’s practices journalists, researchers, and the public analyzed the leaked data, and some sites made it searchable

Hacker / activist
Malicious / reckless (knowingly harmful)
Public leak
Data published; litigation and extortion concerns

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Is it acceptable to punish a company for its practices by exposing its users? (ii) Is it ethical for researchers and journalists to analyze or report on stolen personal data? (iii) Do people who used the service deserve less privacy protection?
