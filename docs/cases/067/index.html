Case No.067

Davidson & Associates v. Jung (Battle.net / bnetd reverse engineering)

Legal Boundaries & CFAA Overreach · 2005.0
Chilling effect

Developers reverse-engineered Blizzard's Battle.net gaming service to build 'bnetd,' a free open-source server that let players run their own game servers. Blizzard sued under the DMCA and breach of the end-user license agreement (EULA). The Eighth Circuit ruled against the developers, finding that the EULA's prohibition on reverse engineering was enforceable and that reverse engineering to achieve interoperability did not qualify for the DMCA's interoperability exception because the developers had agreed to the EULA. The case chilled open-source interoperability work.

Independent / contractor
Good faith (intended public benefit)
None (withheld)
Civil suit / Injunction

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Can an EULA contractually waive the DMCA's statutory right to reverse engineer for interoperability?; (ii) Is building a compatible open-source alternative to a proprietary service a legitimate security/research activity or IP infringement?; (iii) Should agreeing to a ToS/EULA strip a researcher of rights they would otherwise have under the DMCA's interoperability exception?; (iv) How does this ruling affect security researchers who need to reverse-engineer software they've licensed to find vulnerabilities?
