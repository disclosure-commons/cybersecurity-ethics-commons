Case No.160

Kaseya VSA ransomware supply-chain attack

Critical Infrastructure & Public Trust · 2021

in July 2021 the REvil ransomware group exploited Kaseya’s VSA remote-management software the malicious update was pushed through managed service providers (MSPs) to their downstream customers, including many small businesses researchers at the Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure (DIVD) had reported the vulnerability to Kaseya in good faith before the attack Kaseya was reportedly still working on a patch when the attack happened the FBI reportedly had access to REvil’s infrastructure and obtained a decryption key but held it back for about three weeks the reported reason was to avoid tipping off REvil while planning a disruption operation, while victims stayed locked out REvil’s servers went offline shortly after, and Kaseya later got a key from a third party

Cybercriminal group
Malicious / reckless (knowingly harmful)
None (withheld)
Ransomware incident; recovery and law-enforcement actions

Key ethical dilemmas

(i) Should law enforcement hold back a decryption key to pursue the attackers, or give it to victims right away? (ii) How long is too long for a vendor to fix a vulnerability that researchers reported in good faith? (iii) Who decides whose harm counts more: current victims or future ones?
